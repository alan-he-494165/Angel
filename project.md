# Sparse NQS-Supervised Fine-Tuning of Machine-Learned Interatomic Potentials Using Frozen-Core FCI Hamiltonians

## 1. Project Vision

This project develops a sparse neural quantum state (NQS)-supervised fine-tuning protocol for adapting pretrained machine-learned interatomic potentials (MLIPs) to noncovalent interaction (NCI) landscapes. The NQS teacher is trained without reference energy labels: for each selected molecular geometry, the electronic-structure problem is written as a frozen-core FCI Hamiltonian, and the NQS is optimized variationally by minimizing the energy expectation value.

The target is not to claim a universally better MLIP. Instead, the project asks whether sparse neural-wavefunction supervision can affordably specialize existing MLIPs for weak interactions, especially pi-pi stacking, where small errors in dispersion, exchange-repulsion, electrostatics, and polarization can change the shape and ranking of interaction landscapes.

Core hypothesis:

> A pretrained MLIP can be made more accurate for correlation-sensitive NCI landscapes by fine-tuning it on a compact set of configurations whose correction labels are generated by variational NQS solvers on frozen-core FCI Hamiltonians, rather than by relabeling full molecular dynamics trajectories with expensive high-level electronic-structure methods.

## 2. One-Sentence Contribution

We propose a sparse NQS-supervised fine-tuning protocol that uses label-free variational NQS solutions of frozen-core FCI Hamiltonians to generate neural-wavefunction correction signals for selected NCI-critical configurations.

## 3. Motivation

Most practical MLIPs are trained on density functional theory (DFT) or semiempirical quantum chemistry data. These labels are affordable and scalable, but for noncovalent interactions their systematic errors can be chemically meaningful. This is especially true for dispersion-dominated and mixed interactions such as pi-pi stacking, where energy differences of less than 1 kcal/mol can determine equilibrium distance, relative motif stability, and structural preference.

Common trajectory-based fine-tuning improves configuration coverage. It does not necessarily improve label fidelity if the trajectory and labels come from the same approximate electronic-structure level or from the pretrained MLIP itself. In that setting, fine-tuning may reinforce the original bias.

This project separates three roles:

- inexpensive MD, pretrained MLIP uncertainty, or active learning identifies important configurations;
- frozen-core FCI Hamiltonians define the electronic problem for those configurations;
- NQS is trained variationally on those Hamiltonians and supplies sparse neural-wavefunction-level correction signals;
- fine-tuning adapts the pretrained MLIP toward a more accurate NCI landscape.

## 4. Research Questions

1. Can sparse correction signals from variational NQS solvers improve the NCI accuracy of a pretrained MLIP under a fixed NQS solving budget?
2. Is NQS-supervised fine-tuning more data-efficient than ordinary trajectory-based fine-tuning with DFT-level or base-model labels?
3. Which fine-tuning strategy is most stable: full fine-tuning, frozen-layer fine-tuning, adapter-style tuning, or a separate delta-correction head?
4. Does the improvement appear in landscape properties, not only pointwise energy errors?
5. Can the protocol transfer from small aromatic dimers to larger pi-stacked systems such as substituted aromatics, DNA base-pair stacking, or L7-like complexes?

## 6. Positioning and Novelty

The project should be framed as a protocol paper, not as a foundation-model paper.

Existing work already shows that:

- MLIPs can learn DFT-level molecular potential energy surfaces;
- transfer learning can move MLIPs toward coupled-cluster accuracy;
- NCI-specific neural models can use SAPT decomposition or pairwise physical structure;
- long-range physics is important for molecular MLIPs;
- neural quantum states can approximate molecular wavefunctions and potential energy surfaces in selected settings.

The novelty here is the combination:

> Use variationally trained NQS solvers on frozen-core FCI Hamiltonians as sparse neural-wavefunction teachers for fine-tuning pretrained MLIPs on NCI-critical configurations.

The paper should avoid the claim:

> We propose an absolutely better MLIP.

Instead, the claim should be:

> We propose an affordable, sparse, variational NQS-supervised adaptation protocol for weak-interaction landscapes.

## 8. Proposed Protocol

### Step 1: Choose a Pretrained Base MLIP

Primary choice:

- MACE-OFF

Optional comparisons:

- ANI-2x
- ANI-1ccx
- AIMNet2

The base model should be evaluated before any fine-tuning to identify where it fails on NCI landscapes.

### Step 2: Build NCI-Critical Configuration Sets

Use a combination of:

- benchmark structures from S66x8;
- DES370K pi-stacking and aromatic subsets;
- generated benzene dimer scans;
- substituted benzene dimer scans;
- DNA base-pair stacking geometries;
- active-learning selections from base-model uncertainty.

The selected configurations should cover:

- compressed repulsive region;
- equilibrium region;
- dissociation tail;
- lateral sliding;
- rotation between stacking motifs;
- sandwich, slipped-parallel, and T-shaped motifs.

### Step 3: Solve Frozen-Core FCI Hamiltonians with NQS

For each selected geometry, construct the frozen-core electronic Hamiltonian in a determinant/configuration basis. The NQS is then trained without external energy labels by variationally minimizing the Hamiltonian expectation value:

```text
|psi_theta> = sum_x psi_theta(x) |x>

E(theta) = <psi_theta|H_FCI-frozen-core|psi_theta> / <psi_theta|psi_theta>
```

The resulting optimized NQS supplies a sparse correction signal for MLIP fine-tuning. In this document, "NQS label" means an energy or force-related quantity produced by this variational NQS solver, not a supervised label used to train the NQS itself.

Initial NQS-derived signal targets:

- interaction energy;
- relative energy along scan coordinates;
- uncertainty/error bar from stochastic optimization;
- optional force projections along separation or sliding coordinates.

Candidate NQS tools:

- DeepQMC;
- FermiNet-style real-space neural wavefunctions;
- PauliNet-style neural wavefunctions;
- NAQS-style second-quantized neural wavefunctions.

Important controls:

- define frozen-core choices, orbital basis, and active spaces consistently;
- compute dimer and monomer energies under matched basis and frozen-core conventions;
- compare NQS-derived interaction energies against CCSD(T)/CBS or trusted benchmark values on small systems;
- report NQS variance or uncertainty;
- avoid claiming NQS is a universal oracle.

### Step 4: Fine-Tune the MLIP

Compare several adaptation strategies:

| Strategy | Description | Expected Benefit |
|---|---|---|
| Full fine-tuning | Update all model parameters | Highest flexibility, highest forgetting risk. |
| Frozen-layer fine-tuning | Freeze most layers and update final layers | Lower risk and more stable small-data behavior. |
| Delta-correction head | Train a correction on top of base MLIP: E_final = E_base + Delta E_NQS | Most aligned with sparse supervision. |
| Pairwise NCI correction head | Add a correction focused on intermolecular atom pairs | Better physical structure for NCI. |
| Adapter-style tuning | Add small trainable modules while keeping base model fixed | Parameter-efficient and easier to compare. |

Recommended first implementation:

> Keep the pretrained MACE-OFF model fixed and train a small delta-correction head on sparse NQS interaction-energy labels.

This reduces catastrophic forgetting and makes the protocol easier to analyze.

### Step 5: Evaluate Landscape Accuracy

Do not rely only on pointwise energy MAE.

Primary metrics:

- interaction energy MAE/RMSE;
- binding energy error;
- equilibrium distance error;
- motif ranking accuracy;
- curve-shape error along separation scans;
- sliding/rotation landscape error;
- long-range tail behavior;
- compressed-region repulsion behavior;
- force projection error if force labels are available;
- catastrophic forgetting on general organic conformations.

Key comparisons:

- base MLIP;
- MD trajectory fine-tuning with low-level labels;
- DFT-level fine-tuning;
- CCSD(T)/SAPT fine-tuning if available;
- NQS-supervised fine-tuning;
- random NQS selection vs active NQS selection.

- substituted benzene dimer scans;

### MVP Base Model

- MACE-OFF as the main pretrained MLIP.

### MVP NQS Teacher

- Construct frozen-core FCI Hamiltonians for a small set of dimers and scan geometries.
- Train NQS variationally on those Hamiltonians, without FCI-supervised energy labels.
- Validate the resulting NQS interaction curves against available CCSD(T)/CBS or literature reference values.

### MVP Fine-Tuning

- Fixed base MLIP plus delta-correction head.
- Label budgets: 25, 50, 100, 250, 500 configurations solved by the variational NQS teacher.

### MVP Results

Show that sparse NQS-supervised fine-tuning improves:

- pi-stacking curve shape;
- equilibrium distance;
- motif ranking;
- interaction energy MAE;
- transfer to unseen aromatic dimers;
- data efficiency relative to random trajectory fine-tuning.

## 11. Risks and Mitigations

### Risk 1: Variational NQS Energies Are Too Noisy for Weak Interaction Energies

Mitigation:

- begin with small systems where reference values exist;
- train across geometry scans using a consistent protocol;
- report uncertainty bars;
- focus on relative curves before claiming absolute binding energies;
- use benchmark CCSD(T)/CBS values as calibration.

### Risk 2: NQS Is Too Expensive for Benzene-Scale Systems

Mitigation:

- start with ethene, acetylene, small aromatic fragments, and reduced-basis benzene;
- use NQS only for sparse selected points and compact frozen-core Hamiltonians;
- keep CCSD(T)/SAPT references as calibration;
- frame large-system results as transfer tests rather than direct NQS-solved training cases.

### Risk 3: Fine-Tuning Causes Catastrophic Forgetting

Mitigation:

- prefer delta-correction head or adapter-style tuning;
- keep base MLIP frozen in the first version;
- include general organic validation after fine-tuning;
- regularize the correction to vanish outside NCI-relevant regions.

### Risk 4: Reviewer Says the Method Is Just FCI or CCSD(T) Relabeling

Mitigation:

- state clearly that NQS is not supervised by FCI labels;
- explain that "FCI" refers to the frozen-core Hamiltonian representation, not exact diagonalization labels;
- explicitly compare variational NQS supervision to CCSD(T)/SAPT on small systems;
- position NQS as a label-free neural-wavefunction solver and sparse MLIP teacher;
- emphasize sparse adaptation protocol rather than universal superiority;
- show at least one case where NQS-supervised fine-tuning improves the base MLIP under a fixed small-data budget.

### Risk 5: Improvement Comes From Better Sampling, Not Variational NQS Supervision

Mitigation:

- use same configurations with different labels;
- compare low-level label fine-tuning vs NQS label fine-tuning;
- run random selection vs active selection at equal NQS solving budgets.

## 15. Success Criteria

The project is successful if it demonstrates all of the following:

- sparse variational NQS correction signals can be generated with controlled uncertainty for selected NCI configurations;
- NQS-supervised fine-tuning improves at least one pretrained MLIP on pi-stacking landscapes;
- improvement appears in landscape properties, not only energy MAE;
- the protocol is more label-efficient than dense trajectory relabeling or random fine-tuning;
- the adapted model does not catastrophically forget general organic chemistry;
- the paper presents the method as a reusable protocol rather than a universal replacement for existing MLIPs.

## 16. Preferred Final Claim

> Sparse variational NQS supervision provides an affordable route for adapting pretrained MLIPs to weak-interaction landscapes by solving frozen-core FCI Hamiltonians only on configurations where DFT-trained potentials are most likely to inherit chemically meaningful noncovalent-interaction errors.

## 17. Claims to Avoid

- NQS is always more accurate than CCSD(T).
- NQS is always cheaper than conventional quantum chemistry.
- The NQS teacher is trained from FCI reference labels.
- The fine-tuned model is universally better than existing MLIPs.
- MD trajectory fine-tuning is obsolete.
- Pi-stacking performance alone proves general NCI accuracy.
